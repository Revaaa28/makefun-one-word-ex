# Candidates — "One Word From Your Ex" (u-brave-eagle-12-one-word-from-your-ex-cfd97ba2)

Manual selection only. No Gemini/NVIDIA judge, no judge-API/proxy tournament.
Ranked against the kit judge brief's criteria IN THEIR STATED ORDER:

1. Exactly one word an ex would really use to describe a former partner.
2. Makes the judge laugh out loud (surprise, sharp turn, lands harder than expected).
3. Stings with recognition — painfully true, and the entrant plainly knows it.
4. Lands instantly, needs no explanation, cannot be swapped for a near synonym
   without losing its edge.
5. Relatable — a word people repeat because they have lived it too.
Plus: "a word that stings because it is true beats a word that only insults;
meanness for its own sake does not count."

## Pairwise checks vs strong rival-style words

Likely strong field entries: "clingy", "toxic", "boring", "selfish", "needy",
"controlling", "immature", "forgettable", "cheap", "lazy".

- **mid vs clingy**: both pass C1. C2 decides: "clingy" is the expected ex
  complaint (no surprise); "mid" is a verdict on your entire existence in
  three letters — bigger laugh, bigger sting. **mid wins.**
- **mid vs toxic**: "toxic" is pure insult (the brief discounts meanness for its
  own sake) and expected; "mid" is funnier and more specific. **mid wins.**
- **mid vs forgettable**: "forgettable" stings but is a slow, sad word; "mid"
  lands instantly and is funnier. **mid wins.**
- **mid vs boring**: "boring" is generic and expected; "mid" is the same
  verdict with slang punch and surprise. **mid wins.**
- **mid vs rebound**: the closest fight. "rebound" has a great sharp turn
  (C2) and sting (C3), but it describes the relationship's role, not you —
  and it needs the ex to have had someone before you. "mid" describes YOU,
  needs no backstory, lands faster (C4), and is more universal (C5).
  **mid wins, narrowly.**
- **mid vs almost**: "almost" is poetic and stings (C3), but it makes the
  judge sad, not laugh (C2) — and the brief weights laugh second.
  **mid wins.**
- **mid vs practice**: "practice" has a great turn, but as a descriptor it is
  slightly off ("he was practice" works, but it is a noun forced into an
  adjective's job); "mid" is a natural descriptor. C1 + C4 decide.
  **mid wins.**

## Decision

**CHOSEN WORD: `mid`**

- 3 bytes — also the smallest text in the field, which wins the kit's exact-tie
  break (smaller text places first).
- Passes C1 cleanly ("he was mid" is a real ex sentence), wins C2 with the
  brutal bathos of a one-syllable total verdict, stings (C3) because it is
  worse than being hated — it is being rated average — lands instantly with no
  synonym keeping the edge (C4: "average" is a report, "mid" is a sentence),
  and is maximally relatable (C5) in current slang.
- Runner-up: `rebound` (kept in mind only; one entry only, no v2 in this task).
